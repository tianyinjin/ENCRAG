Nuclear weapons
The existence of nuclear weapons of proven capability has deterred direct conflict
between the major powers since World War II. The collapse of communism in Eastern
Europe has effectively ended the confrontation between East and West. The signature of
the Charter of Paris in November 1990 formally ended the Cold War, but large nuclear
stockpiles remain. However, fear of superpower imbalance has been replaced by fear of
“proliferation” –the spread of nuclear capability to powers outside the major blocks.
Until 1989-90, the greatest danger of conflict seemed to lie in Europe. Soviet forces
withdrew from Hungary and the former Czechoslovakia in 1991, and are being removed
from Poland and the former East Germany. A number of US bases in Eastern Europe
have also closed. Local or regional conflicts in other parts of the world are now seen as a
greater threat, for example the Iraqi invasion of Kuwait in August 1990 although the civil
wars in former Yugoslavia greatly increased tension in Europe in 1992-93.
=========
Deterrence remains the key element in France’s strategic defence, the safeguard
against any threat to its vital interests, whatever source it may have and whatever
form it may take.
The Strategic Air Command is the airborne component of nuclear deterrence. Until
2018, it used to comprise three squadrons of Mirage 2000N carrying the medium
range air-to-ground missile ASMP and one squadron of C135FR in-flight refuelling
aircraft.
In order to facilitate the operational control of the resources placed at its disposal to
implement the nuclear option, the Strategic Air Command has two operation centres
as well as its own specific communication networks, notably the air group Astarté,
a specialized air transmission unit for relaying orders from the highest authorities
to the SNLEs (sea launched ballistic missile submarines) on patrol. The Strategic
Air Command has also a dedicated Mirage IV unit for strategic reconnaissance.
=========
French nuclear weapons, 2023 :
- France’s nuclear arsenal has remained stable over the past decade and contains
approximately 290 warheads.
- Nearly all of France’s warheads are deployed or operationally available for deployment on
short notice.
- Other than the United States, France is the most transparent of the nuclear-armed states,
having disclosed details about its nuclear forces and operations for many years.
- President François Hollande declared on February 19, 2015 that France had a stockpile
of 300 warheads for “three sets of 16 submarine-based missiles and 54 ASMPA
[medium(cid:2)range air-launched] delivery systems”. President Emmanuel Macron reaffirmed
the Sarkozy formulation of “under 300 nuclear weapons” in a speech on February 7, 2020.
