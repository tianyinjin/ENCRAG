BOEING
STRATEGIC HIGHLIGHTS
Business trends
f Boeing revenues reached $66.5 billion (€61.5 billion) in 2024, down 14.5% year-on-year in local currency. The
Commercial Airplanes division is still facing difficulties: its revenues fell 32.6% year-on-year and only 348 aicraft
were delivered in 2024 compared to 528 in 2023. The group still recorded losses during the year (operating
loss of $10.7 billion, or €9.9 billion), mainly due to the Commercial Airplanes and Defense, Space & Security
divisions, with programmes suffering from major delays (KC-46A Pegasus, VC-25B or MQ-25 Stingray).
f Boeing operates in the military sector through its Defense, Space & Security division, which generated sales
of $23.9 billion (€22.1 billion) in 2024. Deliveries of combat aircraft decreased over the year (11 F/A-18E/F
Super Hornet and 14 F-15E Strike Eagle / F-15EX Eagle II, compared with 22 and 9 respectively in 2023), as
did deliveries of new CH-47 Chinook helicopters (4 in 2024, compared with 11 in 2023), new AH-64 Apache
helicopters (16 in 2024, compared to 20 in 2023), KC-46A Pegasus tanker aircraft (10 in 2024, compared to
13 in 2023) and P-8A Poseidon maritime patrol aircraft (4 in 2024, compared to 11 in 2023). The group also
handed over six MH-139A Grey Wolf helicopters to the U.S. Army (compared to two in 2023).
f In July 2024, Boeing reached an agreement to acquire its subcontractor Spirit AeroSystems (which posted
revenues of $6.3 billion, or €5.8 billion, in 2024), for $4.7 billion (€4.3 billion). With Spirit AeroSystems being
responsible for producing defective parts for civil aircraft (notably the 737 MAX), Boeing seeks to bring part of
its aircraft production back in-house to better detect any manufacturing defects. The group intends to take over
three factories in the United States and two in the United Kingdom by the end of 2025.
Home market
f The group generated 54% of its turnover in the United States in 2024. In defence, the U.S. remains by far
Boeing’s largest market (78% of sales).
f In January 2025, the U.S. Air Force announced the award in 2026 of a contract for the production of the first
batch of T-7A Red Hawk advanced trainer aircraft (produced by Boeing and Saab). The aircraft should reach
initial operational capability (IOC) in late 2027 or early 2028.
f In March 2025, the U.S. Air Force selected Boeing, over Lockheed Martin, to develop the future sixth-generation
F-47 air superiority aircraft (NGAD programme), which will be accompanied by UCAVs. This aircraft is intended
to replace the F-22 Raptor fighter aircraft (Lockheed Martin).
f In July 2025, Boeing secured a $2.8 billion (€2.6 billion) contract with the U.S. Space Force to develop and
produce two communication, command and control satellites (with an option for two more), enabling the
President of the United States to use nuclear weapons in all circumstances.
Export markets
f The group generated 46% of its revenues from exports in 2024. About half of exports sales were generated by
the Commercial Airplanes division.
f Boeing was awarded two contracts worth a combined $580 million (€536 million) in December 2024 by the
U.S. Air Force to integrate new electronic systems on 68 F-15J Eagle fighter aircraft belonging to the Japan Self-
Defense Forces (Japan Super Interceptor programme). The group will supply its new AN/ALQ-250 EPAWSS
protection system (warning radar, countermeasures).
f In June 2025, Boeing and the Royal Australian Air Force demonstrated the destruction of an aerial target by two
MQ-28 Ghost Bat «loyal wingman» UCAVs controlled from an E-7A Wedgetail detection aircraft.
Technology and Innovation
f The group’s total R&D expenditure reached $3.8 billion (€3.5 billion) in 2024, representing 5.7% of revenues.
