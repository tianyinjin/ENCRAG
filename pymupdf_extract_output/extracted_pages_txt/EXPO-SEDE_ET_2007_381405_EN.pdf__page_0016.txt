An interesting development is the use of UAVs by government agencies other than the military 
and by civilian users. Here large UAVs are seen as useful for SAR, surveillance of large areas 
(e.g. forests or fishing grounds), and geographic and geological survey. Smaller, ‘tactical’ or 
mini UAVs could well be practical for police forces for area surveillance (e.g. for traffic 
control), while even the smallest UAVs are already being offered for small scale agricultural 
use, for example42. 
 
All analysts agree the UAV market will still see strong growth in the coming decade. 
Information from open sources on planned acquisitions globally indicates that most countries 
currently possessing UAVs will expand their inventory and/or improve sensors on their UAVs. 
In addition, many new military users of UAVs will emerge. Some predictions have been made 
about the size of the future global UAV market (for example, one estimate suggests that the 
market will be worth about US$13.5bn43 between 2005 and 2014: another suggests 
US$17.5bn44 between 2003 and 2012), although it is impossible to judge how good these 
stimates are.
