Se présenter 
 
Colonel X, 
  
Greetings from Djibouti 
  
My name is Lieutenant Colonel X X and I’m the U.S. Defense Attaché at the U.S. Embassy here in Djibouti 
  
CJTFHOA forwarded me your contact information and said you would be in Djibouti in mid-November 
  
I’ve been in Djibouti for 16 months, so I’m quite familiar with all things Djibouti and of course Camp Lemonnier 
and CJTFHOA. 
  
I suggest maybe dinner or lunch with myself and the French Defense Attaché, Colonel X X, so we can answer 
any questions you may have concerning Djibouti and Camp Lemonnier. 
  
Best, 
  
LTC X X 
U.S. Army, Cavalry 
SDO/DATT, Djibouti 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Use proper memo format in all correspondence. 
 
Address the officer by title and last name (e.g.  “LT Smith,”) or sir/ma’am (e.g.  “Good afternoon sir,”) in the first 
line of the e-mail. 
 
Sir/ma’am does not need to be written after sentences in the e-mail as it would be used verbally; write as you 
would in a normal business e-mail. 
 
Close the e-mail with “Very respectfully, (skip a line) MIDN class last name.”
