4.1.2 
Performance Differences and Insights across Data Segments for Drone Detection 
Algorithm  
To highlight how such a benchmark would enable a granular and robust investigation of even out-of-the-box, 
non-fine-tuned models, we employ the methods described above to examine if we are able to find the 
potential areas of underperformance of an Ultralytics YOLOE (Real-time Seeing Anything) model 
developed by Ao Wang, Lihao Liu, Hui Chen, Zijia Lin, Jungong Han, and Guiguang Ding. [18] YOLOE 
was designed for open-vocabulary detection and segmentation, and uses text, image, or internal vocabulary 
prompts, enabling real-time detection of any object class. It was selected as its developers were able to 
‘achieve state-of-the-art zero-shot performance with minimal impact on speed and accuracy’ and is likely to 
be archetypal of the architectures used in real-world counter-drone CV system development.  
We achieve the following overall results that show an overall mAP at 0.22 at the IoU threshold of 0.5 for the 
EO and augmented simulator dataset, and a score of 0.04 for the IR dataset. Further investigation into the 
metric split by bounding box areas shows that the model performs much better when the target within the 
image is large (at least 96 x 96 pixels), and the model has almost negligible performance on small targets (32 
x 32 pixels or smaller). Though unsurprising, this validates our hypothesis that there would be performance 
differences in the CV system across small and medium drones, as well as how far the drones would be 
detected away from the sensor, as further objects will appear as smaller given the same sensor field of view. 
Notably, as Wang et al. noted that YOLOE was largely trained on large COCO datasets and further grounded 
by Objects365 (V1) and GoldG datasets, we can infer that the model was not significantly exposed to infra-
red data. This hypothesis is supported by the below metrics showing negligible performance on the infra-red 
segment of our evaluation data.
