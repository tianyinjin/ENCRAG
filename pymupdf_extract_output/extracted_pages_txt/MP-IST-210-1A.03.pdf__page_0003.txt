2.1 
Complexity of Performance Measurement in Networked, Inter-dependent Counter-
Drone Systems 
 
Figure 1: ASUL being presented at BAAINBw in Koblenz. Image was released by the 
 
Bundeswehr for public distribution.
Measuring the performance of CV systems for counter-drone missions can be a complex and multi-
faceted subject. Firstly, contained counter-drone systems are themselves becoming significantly more 
complex, comprising a mix of sensors, effectors, and coordination and processing software, in order 
to match the complexity and diversity of the range of threats they are required to face. For example, 
the Bundeswehr announced in Aug 2022 its deployment of the ASUL counter-drone system, highlighting 
its components [1]: “the sensor system… is composed of… three radars and a camera system with day and 
night vision. A radio frequency (RF) direction finder used to identify control signals and, if possible, also 
locate the position of the drone pilot… Once an aircraft has been detected by the radars and the 
direction finder, the system will compare its data with information available from data bases. In such a 
way, the system determines whether the aircraft could be a drone. After an alleged target has been 
detected, the system’s operator will be informed in real time and will be able to verify, with the help 
of the camera system, if the drone is to be classified as a threat.” While the exact specifications and 
workflows of the system are classified, within one counter-drone system, there is already a variety of 
visual data generated through its radar, electro-optical sensor, and thermal sensors. The system will 
likely also be engineered to handle the inherent variability in size, speed, materials, behavior, and 
payloads of various adversary drones of differing signatures, and function across a range of 
environmental conditions including electromagnetic interference and expected weather phenomena
