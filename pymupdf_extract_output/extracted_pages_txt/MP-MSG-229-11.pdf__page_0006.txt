2.1 
System Components and Architecture 
The STAR macro-level architecture modules are shown in Figure 2, including i) multi-modal, ii) capability 
reckoning and dynamic tasking, all leading to human meaningful control. 
 
Figure 2: STAR Components demonstrated in C2 Decision awareness for Manned-Unmanned 
Teaming in Critical Undersea Infrastructure Protection. 
1) Multi-Modal Pipeline and Pre-Processing: This module processes situational awareness data, 
extracting relevant features for dynamic tasking. It filters and engineers the data, transforming it into 
actionable mission parameters, like trajectory calculations from GPS data. It also allows 
customization based on vehicle types, mission objectives, and operational needs, supporting a 
variety of mission scenarios. 
2) Reckoning: STAR evaluates which agent—human or autonomous—is best suited for a task by 
analyzing both past and current behaviors. It uses data from the multi-modal pipeline, such as 
mission status and environmental sensing, to determine the interdependence between agents. This 
helps identify capacity gaps and whether agents can act independently or need support. 
3) Dynamic Adaptive Tasking: This module focuses on task allocation for manned-unmanned 
teaming (MUM-T). It takes into account pre-operation prerequisites, doctrine, and the commander’s 
requirements while enabling real-time adjustments. It analyzes the interdependence among team 
members (both human and autonomous) and recommends which agent should lead or support based 
on the operational context. The commander can either accept or modify these recommendations and 
adjust the level of autonomy as needed.
