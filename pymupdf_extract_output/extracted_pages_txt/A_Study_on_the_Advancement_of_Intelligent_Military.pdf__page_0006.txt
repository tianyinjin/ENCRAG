This implies that relying solely on the detection neural
network is not the best strategy. Therefore, moving the
drone and adjusting the angle of the camera attached to
it to secure a line of sight on the object can be another
way to enhance the confidence level. However, could
such movements be addressed with a sort of rule-based
approach? Considering the constraints of maintaining
a certain altitude and the drone’s requirement to look
downwards from the air, resolving this issue with a
rule-based approach is not as straightforward as it may
seem. Let’s think of a situation where, if something
like a tree is hiding a tank, the drone has a rule to fly
away from that tree and turn its camera to look at the
tank. For this strategy to work effectively, it’s crucial
to recognize the kinds of objects that could conceal the
tank and figure out which portion of the tank is hidden.
However, training the system to segment and identify
every potential concealing object, such as various trees
and structures, poses a significant challenge, especially
with limited computational resources. Additionally, the
task of detecting a tank that is partly masked is com-
plex, as it requires pinpointing which part of the tank
is covered. While it might look like an easy problem
to solve with basic rules from a human perspective,
for a drone, the situation is much more complex and
not as easy to handle. This is exactly the point where
advanced technology is needed to realize the concept
of intelligence.
Therefore, this paper proposes using reinforcement
learning to solve the issue of securing a line of sight on
incompletely concealed objects and increasing object
confidence levels. For this, we utilize images where
objects have been detected along with various vec-
tor observations to autonomously control the drone’s
movement and the camera angle. In the next chapter,
we delve deeper into the fundamental concepts of re-
inforcement learning, conduct experiments, and then
discuss the results.
C. REINFORCEMENT LEARNING
In reinforcement learning, an agent in the environment, which
is the space where the agent is located, selects actions based
on the current situation information, known as the state. The
environment then changes in response to these actions, and
the agent receives rewards as a result. The goal of reinforce-
ment learning is to maximize the cumulative rewards [46].
In this study, the environment is partially observable from the
agent’s perspective. When the drone scouts for enemies in the
Unity simulator space, it doesn’t initially know the location of
the enemies. It observes only part of the environment using its
camera, not having complete information about the enemies
from the start. This is different from fully observable games
like chess or Go, where the positions and movements of all
pieces are known. In partially observable situations, the term
‘observation’ is used instead of ‘state’ to describe the current
situation information. The following sections introduce the
components of reinforcement learning and the algorithms
used.
1) Environment
In this research, the reinforcement learning aims to achieve
advanced intelligent behaviors in scenarios occurring after
relatively basic tasks like waypoint reconnaissance and en-
emy tracking. Therefore, the reinforcement learning envi-
ronment concentrates on the drone’s autonomous actions to
enhance the detection confidence level after tracking a tank,
especially when the confidence level is low. As depicted in
Figure 7, at the start of learning, the drone is positioned
above a partially obscured tank. The drone receives visual
input through a red camera located on its underside, which is
essential for the detection process. This red box represents the
drone’s "eye." The grey camera and the large grey square are
used to observe the drone’s behavior for verification purposes
and are not directly involved in the learning process.
