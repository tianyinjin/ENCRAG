This paper has the following two limitations. First, there
is a lack of appropriate and fair judgment methods for the
results of reinforcement learning. This indicates that addi-
tional verification and evaluation are needed before applying
these reinforcement learning outcomes in real-world environ-
ments. To overcome this limitation, future research should
concentrate on developing and validating more sophisticated
equitable evaluation methods to enhance the reliability of the
research findings.
Second, there is no confirmation that the series of processes
for intelligent reconnaissance drones can be successfully ap-
plied and function in actual environments. In particular, the
critical aspect of communication, which may present real
challenges, is missing. Therefore, the sim-to-real aspect re-
mains a future task to be explored through procedures like
those shown in Figure 13. This paper represents Step 1,
where experiments are conducted within a simulator. As a
future research task for Step 2, the reinforcement learning
neural network will be implemented on a mission computer
to test its interoperability with the simulator. In Step 3, real
environments and sensors will be used, along with connecting
a flight controller for operation and enabling the monitoring
of drone information through the Ground Control System
(GCS). Through this process, it will be possible to refine
the procedures for intelligent reconnaissance drones to ensure
their suitability for actual environments.
