Le coût de la dronisation en ressources humaines 
P.6
 
Executive Summary 
The phenomenon of the dronisation and robotisation of armed forces—part of a broader 
transformation underpinned by information enhancement and digitalisation—has become a 
central concern for numerous powers, as drones assume a critical role in the ongoing conflict 
between Ukraine and Russia. These systems now perform a wide spectrum of functions along 
the front line, ranging from logistical support to the establishment of a comprehensive "re-
connaissance-strike" model, and have become indispensable to the resurgence of mass in mil-
itary operations. The use of drones has reached such a scale that an estimated 40 to 60 per 
cent of the human resources within Ukrainian brigades are currently engaged in maintaining 
the drone functional chain. As such, production processes, logistical frameworks, and opera-
tional planning must all adapt in order to employ drone systems with the utmost effective-
ness. 
Dronisation affects all components of the armed forces, albeit to varying degrees. The initial 
cost is primarily linked to the establishment of an ecosystem conducive to drone integration—
characterised by intensified digitalisation and enhanced data processing—and to the broader 
cultural adaptation of units directly or indirectly involved. Beyond this foundational ecosys-
tem, the full integration of drones will generate substantial costs across all elements of the 
drone functional chain. A sustainable production capacity must be developed, requiring not 
only the acquisition of appropriate machinery but, more crucially, the recruitment and train-
ing of qualified personnel. From a logistical standpoint, the impact on human resources may 
prove relatively neutral, as robotisation and dronisation could relieve a significant number of 
personnel from certain functions, thereby enabling their reallocation to other operational 
tasks. This redistribution may partially offset the logistical demands associated with drone 
integration. However, the most significant burden appears to lie in processing the vast volume 
of data generated by these systems. The recruitment of analysts and technical specialists ca-
pable of managing and exploiting such data will place a considerable strain on the human 
resources of the armed forces.Furthermore, the effective integration of drones will necessitate 
the recruitment and training of skilled personnel for both piloting and maintaining these sys-
tems. 
These considerations should prompt reflection on the development of a hybrid integration 
model—combining the generalised adoption of drones across all units with the establishment 
of specialised formations tasked with well-defined missions. It is precisely this balance be-
tween the two approaches that will determine the overall cost of dronisation for the armed 
forces. In addition to the pursuit of operational excellence in drone deployment, it is essential 
to foster a culture of experimentation and innovation around these emerging capabilities.
