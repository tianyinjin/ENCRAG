3.3 
Dynamic Tasking of MUM-T in MDO  
The algorithm presented here addresses a dynamic, energy-constrained decision-making problem for 
autonomous agents operating in complex environments. The agent, modeled with capabilities for target 
detection, observation, and engagement, is tasked with navigating towards a final destination while 
optimizing energy consumption. The system operates by reading a JSON configuration file, which details the 
agent’s energy levels, capabilities, and environmental factors such as observation and engagement range. 
The agent’s reasoning process is driven by continuous updates of its energy state, simulating time 
progression and movement through a series of predefined positions determined during mission planning. As 
the agent moves, it computes the energy required to reach subsequent positions or the final destination, 
adjusting its route if it detects insufficient energy to proceed. At each decision point, the algorithm checks 
whether the agent’s current energy level is adequate for reaching the target positions, considering both 
observation and engagement capabilities. If the agent’s energy falls below the required threshold to reach the 
final destination, it reassesses its route, discarding positions that are unreachable. If the agent has enough 
energy to proceed, it makes decisions based on the optimal route and energy consumption. This decision-
making is extended to multi-agent scenarios, where the algorithm allows for the sharing of information 
regarding target observations and the assignment of tasks based on the least energy-expensive agent. In cases 
where observations are unreachable by any agent, they are flagged and excluded from the task list. The 
algorithm’s ability to dynamically adjust to changing energy levels and environmental conditions, while 
considering agent capabilities and task prioritization, showcases its potential for real-time, energy-efficient 
decision-making in autonomous systems and MUM-T coordination.
