3.1 
Use-Case: Critical Undersea Infrastructure Protection  
Hybrid threats in the maritime domain are evolving, with covert attacks on undersea infrastructure becoming 
a major concern. The Nord Stream pipeline sabotage and Baltic Connector pipeline damage highlight the 
challenges in distinguishing between accidental and deliberate actions, especially when attackers use civilian 
vessels or covert tactics. The rise of Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) and specialized sabotage 
vessels, such as those used by Russia, further complicates protection of critical undersea infrastructure 
(CUI). At the 2023 NATO Vilnius Summit, allies established the Maritime Centre for the Security of Critical 
Underwater Infrastructure within MARCOM, focusing on deterring and defending against hybrid threats 
targeting energy and communication networks. This includes addressing incidents like the Nord Stream 
attack, the damage to the Baltic Connector pipeline and data cables between Finland and Estonia in October 
2023, and ongoing threats to Norwegian gas pipelines and fiber-optic cables, underscoring NATO’s 
commitment to securing Europe’s vital undersea assets.  
3.2 
Modeling & Simulation and System Integration 
This use-case models the defense of critical undersea infrastructure against a range of asymmetric threats in a 
contested maritime environment. The Red Team simulates a series of offensive actions aimed at disrupting 
or destroying vital subsea assets, including communication cables, offshore platforms, and sensor networks. 
These actions include the deployment of UUVs from submarines or surface vessels to sabotage subsea 
cables, cyberattacks to compromise control systems and exfiltrate data via UUVs, signal jamming using 
surface drones or UUVs to disrupt communication, and direct physical assaults on offshore platforms. In 
response, the Blue Team employs a layered defense strategy that integrates advanced assets for detection, 
interdiction, and neutralization of threats. The defense relies on UAVs, such as the Camcopter S-100, for 
aerial surveillance and signal jamming detection, while Iver-3 UUVs perform subsea patrols to inspect 
cables and intercept hostile UUVs. Submarines are deployed for long-range sonar detection and 
counterattacks, supported by swarm drones for persistent coverage and jammer localization. Frigates provide 
surface surveillance and anti-submarine warfare (ASW) capabilities, while RHIBs enable rapid deployment 
for personnel extraction and anti-sabotage operations. The use-case examines the interplay between these 
assets to evaluate force readiness and response capabilities in the face of cyber, physical, and electronic 
warfare tactics aimed at critical undersea infrastructure. The use-case uses the same location as Rempus and 
Dynamic messenger which occurs in Troia, Portugal.
