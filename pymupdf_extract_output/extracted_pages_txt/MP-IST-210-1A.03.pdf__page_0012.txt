3.1.1.2 
Translating Desired Technical Qualities into Quantitative Metrics 
Thereafter, the desired technical qualities of the CV solution would have to then be translated into 
quantitative metrics, in order to construct observable parameters to quantify each test protocol and metric. To 
implement this, we model the deployment environment with three primary sources of variability that a CV 
system would have to perform under: at the sensor-level, the target-level, and the encapsulating environment. 
In so doing, we are able to break down the analysis components into understanding the following: detection 
and classification performance, referring to the ability of the system to detect and distinguish the expected 
variety of drones in the area of operations from other drones as well as other expected objects, tracking 
performance, referring to the ability of the system to keep track on a target of interest through time and space 
once identified. In so doing, we will have to further understand the above within more fine-grained 
contextual qualities such as system-to-target fit, referring to the ability of the system to operate well across 
targets of differing physical types or sizes, environmental robustness, referring to the ability of the system to 
consistently perform across the expected environmental conditions of its deployment, sensor behavior 
tolerance, referring to the ability of the system to consistently perform across the expected physical 
behaviors of the sensing platform, e.g. if it were deployed on a robotics or autonomous vehicle, and target 
behavior tolerance, referring to the ability of the system to operate well across targets’ positions and 
movement patterns as captured by the sensor.  
In this effort, the normalizability of parameters is vital, ensuring that performance scores can be scaled and 
compared consistently across different CV systems, sensor types, or operational environments. This allows 
for fair benchmarking and facilitates informed decision-making for procurement and integration, enabling a 
standardized language for evaluating diverse solutions within the counter-drone mission.
