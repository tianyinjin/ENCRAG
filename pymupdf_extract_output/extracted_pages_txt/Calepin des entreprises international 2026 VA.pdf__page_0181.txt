RTX
Business trends
	
 RTX revenues reached $80.7 billion (€74.6 billion) in 2024, 54% of which from defence activities, mainly 
through its Raytheon division. The group is the world’s second-largest defence company by revenues, behind 
Lockheed Martin, and the fourth-largest aerospace company (civil and military) through its Collins Aerospace 
and Pratt & Whitney divisions, behind Boeing, Airbus and AVIC.
	
 In July 2025, the group completed the sale to Safran of Collins Aerospace’s activities in the production and 
maintenance of flight control and actuation systems for aircraft (civil and military) and helicopters for $1.8 billion 
(€1.7 billion).
	
 Raytheon signed a letter of intent with Diehl Defence in August 2025 for the co-production in Europe of 
components for the FIM-92 Stinger air defence missile.
Home market
	
 RTX generated 57% of its revenues on the U.S. market in 2024. The group is a preferred partner of the 
U.S. Department of Defense, alongside Lockheed Martin and Boeing.
	
 In February 2025, Boeing awarded Collins Aerospace a contract for the supply of 144 ACES 5 ejection seats 
to equip the U.S. fleet of F-15EX Eagle II fighter aircraft.
	
 Raytheon signed a $580 million (€536 million) contract in May 2025 with the U.S. Navy to continue production 
of the new NGJ-MB (Next Generation Jammer Mid-Band) suite for the U.S. Navy and Royal Australian Air 
Force’s EA-18G Growler electronic warfare aircraft (Boeing).
	
 In June 2025, Raytheon won a $1.1 billion (€1 billion) contract from the U.S. Navy to produce AIM-9X Sidewinder 
Block II air-to-air missiles. 2,500 missiles will be produced per year under this contract.
	
 In August 2025, the U.S. Navy awarded Pratt & Whitney a $2.8 billion (€2.6 billion) contract to produce 
141 F135 turbojet engines for the F-35 fighter aircraft in batch 18, to be delivered by February 2028.
	
 Raytheon received $205 million (€189 million) in September 2025 from the U.S. Navy to continue production of 
the naval Phalanx CIWS (Close-In Weapon System) defence system until 2029.
	
 In September 2025, the U.S. Army awarded Raytheon a $1.7 billion (€1.6 billion) contract to produce nine 
LTAMDS air and missile defence radars for the U.S. Army and Poland (for the Polish Wisla system).
Export markets
	
 Export sales accounted for 43% of total revenues in 2024 and mostly concerned civil activities. In the military 
sector, the group is benefiting from international demand for Patriot and NASAMS air defence and missile 
defence systems (the NASAMS system was developed in partnership with Kongsberg).
	
 In January 2025, Romania ordered additional Patriot missile defence systems (radars, control stations and 
interceptor missiles) worth $946 million (€874 million). The Netherlands also ordered a Patriot system and 
associated equipment, worth $529 million (€489 million), to replace the unit sold to Ukraine.
	
 In June 2025, Raytheon received a $250 million (€231 million) order from Mitsubishi Electric for the supply of 
RIM-162 ESSM Block 2 naval interceptor missile kits and components, which will be assembled locally under 
licence by the Japanese group.
	
 In October 2025, the German Navy selected Raytheon’s AN/SPY-6(V)1 AESA naval air and missile defence 
radar for installation on the eight future F127 frigates.
Technology and Innovation
	
 Self-financed R&D expenditure amounted to $2.9 billion (€2.7 billion) in 2024, representing 3.6% of revenues.
	
 In June 2025, Raytheon and Northrop Grumman conducted tests on the engine of the Next-Generation Short-
Range Interceptor (NGSRI) missile, intended to replace the FIM-92 Stinger in service with the U.S. Army.
