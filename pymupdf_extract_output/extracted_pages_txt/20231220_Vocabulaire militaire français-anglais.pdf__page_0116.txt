94 1MC = One Main Circuit, is the term for the shipboard public address circuits on naval vessels of the 
United States. This provides a means of transmitting general information and orders to all internal ship 
spaces and topside areas, and is loud enough that all embarked personnel are able to hear it. 
Usage examples : 
« General Quarters* ! General Quarters ! All hands [= tous les hommes**] man your battle stations ! » 
« Reveille ! Reveille ! Reveille ! All hands heave out and trice up ! [= Branle-bas !] Reveille ! » 
« Taps ! Taps ! Lights out ! Taps ! » 
« Darken ship ! Make Darken Ship reports to CCS*** » 
« Flight Quarters**** ! Flight Quarters ! Set condition 1-alpha for flight operations ! All personnel remove soft 
hats ! All hands man your Flight Quarters stations !» 
« The following is a test of the helo crash alarm from the flight deck – disregard this alarm. Test complete – 
regard all further alarms ! » 
 
* “General Quarters !” (US Navy) = “Action Stations ! ” (Royal Navy) = Aux postes de combat ! (autrefois 
“branle-bas de combat !”). 
** She was lost with all hands = il a coulé avec tous les hommes à bord / tout l'équipage. (“She” est employé 
pour souligner l’affection et le respect que portent les marins à leur bateau). 
*** Central control station. 
**** “Flight Quarters !” = a ship configuration that assigns and stations personnel at critical positions to 
conduct safe flight operations. 
95 Life saving procedure for sweeping for IEDs using visual checks for ground sign and metal detectors. 
Op Barma = clearing mines and IEDs in front of vehicles or foot patrols.  
To “OP BAR(A)MA” means to make sure there are no secondary explosive devices (after IED-Strike or Find). 
96 A police dog, often referred to as a "K-9 dog" in some areas (which is a homophone of “canine”).
