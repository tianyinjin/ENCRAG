Danger, Response, Airway - Breathing and Circulation (DRABC) 
• What must you do when a member of your squad has been wounded? 
First of all, you must pay attention not to put yourself, the others and the victim in danger. You must 
assess/evaluate the victim and then, you must prepare the medical evacuation radio procedure. Finally, you 
have to give first aid to the victim. However, the two last steps can be done simultaneously. 
• What are the questions that must come to mind? 
Check for responsiveness: if he / she is conscious. If the victim is conscious, check if he/she is alert or 
confused with questions such as “How do you feel?”, “Where does it hurt?” or “How strong is the pain?”. If 
the victim is unconscious, place him / her in lateral safety position. 
• Is the airway clear or noisy? Are there potential obstructions? 
If so you have to clear it. Afterwards, check the victim’s breathing, watch if the chest rises, listen for the 
sound of breathing. 
• What about circulation? 
Check the pulse. Is it strong and / or regular? Is there a haemorrhage? If so, you have to put pressure on 
pressure points, to apply a tourniquet or to put a dressing on the wound. 
If neither breathing nor pulse are present, start the mouth-to-mouth resuscitation (also called kiss of life) 
and the cardiac massage.
