NICHOLAS SCHOOL OF THE ENVIRONMENT
madeforthis.duke.edu
Founded in 1938 as one of the first marine field stations in the U.S., and the first university-based 
marine lab in North Carolina, the Duke University Marine Lab has evolved into Duke’s epicenter 
for immersive field experiences and interdisciplinary research to protect our precious marine 
ecosystems. The Marine Lab operates year-round on Piver’s Island in Beaufort, North Carolina, to 
provide educational training and research opportunities to more than 3,000 individuals annually, 
including Duke students, visiting student groups, area residents, and scientists from around the world. 
The Marine Lab lies within the Nicholas 
School of the Environment at Duke 
University—the first environmental school 
in the world—which offers a professional 
graduate program focused on environmental 
science, management, and policy. The Marine 
Lab’s location on the southern Outer Banks 
of North Carolina allows it to act as Duke’s 
“window to the sea,” providing information 
on the rich environmental mosaic of coastal 
habitats where land meets the water.

MADE FOR ADVANCING OCEAN HEALTH AND POLICY SOLUTIONS. 
The Duke University Marine Lab focuses on solving the problems facing not only marine 
environments but also the people and communities who depend on them. Its scholars are some of 
the most respected experts in their fields, and are sought by local, tribal and federal governments 
as well as the United Nations for impartial scientific advice on the climate crisis, global ocean 
governance, fisheries management, coastal resilience, and marine species conservation.
The Nicholas School and the Duke Marine Lab 
exemplify interdisciplinary scholarship, 
spanning more than 40 disciplines across 
16 focus areas. Leveraging its research 
strengths in oceans, energy transformation, 
climate science, forestry, and planetary, 
environmental and human health, the school 
uses interdisciplinary collaborations and 
advanced technology to enhance 
understanding of complex environmental 
changes and their impacts. The Nicholas School 
and the Duke Marine Lab also translate these 
scientific advances into innovative and scalable 
environmental solutions.
EMPOWERING THE BOLDEST THINKING
We consistently strive to strengthen our 
expertise in marine ecology, conservation, and 
governance by offering named endowed 
professorships to support new faculty 
positions and attract and retain the brightest 
and most innovative academic scholars. We 
also plan to launch the Rachel Carson 
Postdoctoral Scholars program to support early 
career researchers. The program will recruit 
postdoctoral scholars to work with Duke 
Marine Lab faculty for a two-year term, with a 
focus on young scientists from first-generation 
backgrounds. 
TRANSFORMING TEACHING & LEARNING
The Undergraduate Research Scholars 
Program gives Duke undergraduate students 
hands-on, immersive research experiences. The 
program offers guidance from a faculty mentor, 
funding for travel and conferences, and 
professional development workshops, 
culminating in an honors thesis for graduation 
with distinction. At the graduate level, many 
gifted students defer their professional 
education because of financial constraints. Our 
Nicholas Scholars Program will fully support 
the most promising applicants to the Coastal 
and Marine Systems concentration of the 
Master’s of Environmental Management (MEM) 
professional degree program.
STRENGTHENING CAMPUS COMMUNITY
The Marine Lab supports research, teaching and 
visitors from around the world throughout the 
year, but due to constant activity and the harsh 
coastal environment, our older facilities need 
upgrades. Renovating buildings and 
modernizing areas of the Lab would allow the 
Nicholas School to continue its mission of 
education, research, and serving communities 
both locally and globally.

PARTNERING WITH PURPOSE 
The daunting challenges we face over the next 
century will require an inclusive, global 
perspective and sustained local engagement. 
The Marine Lab has a long history of 
partnering with the Carteret County 
community through the Community Science 
Initiative, which aims to bring marine research 
to K–12 students in local school systems. 
Students learn about preventing debris in 
local waterways, monitoring plastic in 
stormwater drains, recovering after major 
weather events, and pursuing future careers in 
marine science and community engagement. 
ENSURING MARINE SCIENCE LEADERSHIP 
Establishing a named new endowment for the 
Marine Lab director position is critical to our 
continued leadership in marine science and 
conservation. With this position permanently 
funded, the Marine Lab can maintain a long-
term strategic vision, avoiding disruption from 
financial constraints. This consistency is 
especially important in fostering marine science 
research, which often involves multidecade 
projects related to climate change, conservation, 
and ocean ecology.
